Recent field notes

Where dispatch teams lose time between the call and the schedule

Short answer: The gap usually sits between intake, scheduling, and status updates, where the same detail is copied across calls, texts, calendars, and work orders.

Start with one week of delayed jobs. Record the handoff, the owner, and the next action. Repeated re-entry is a candidate for a clearer checklist or system handoff, not automatically a new app.

How to spot repeat vendor exceptions before they become customer problems

Short answer: An exception becomes expensive when the team finds it after a customer, owner, or technician has already felt its impact.

Track repeat exceptions by source, time to notice, rework, and final owner. That pattern shows whether the first fix is better visibility, a vendor standard, or a clear escalation rule.

When automation should support an operator, not replace one

Short answer: Automate predictable updates and reminders; keep judgment, customer recovery, and unusual exceptions with a person.

An automation is ready when the trigger, owner, data, and fallback are clear. If the team cannot explain what happens when information is missing, document the workflow before automating it.
