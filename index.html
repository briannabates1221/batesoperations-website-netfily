Do roofing contractors need a written hazard communication program?

Yes, if your employees may be exposed to hazardous chemicals on the job. For roofers that usually means roof primers, adhesives, sealants, coatings, solvents, fuels and aerosol cans. OSHA's construction rule (29 CFR 1926.59) applies the Hazard Communication Standard, 29 CFR 1910.1200, which requires a written program, a list of the hazardous chemicals on hand, container labels, safety data sheets your crew can reach, and training.

OSHA revised the standard in 2024. Employers had until November 20, 2026 to update their written program, labels and training for substances, as necessary. Mixtures follow by May 19, 2028.

Why a template isn't enough

Free HazCom templates are easy to find. The hard part is the part a template leaves blank: your actual product list, where your SDSs live on a jobsite, how you label the bucket a crew decants into, and how you share hazard info with the general contractor.

The written program has to describe what your company actually does. A generic document with someone else's chemicals in it doesn't do that. We fill in every blank from your information so the program matches your crews.

The HazCom 2024 dates

  • May 20, 2024: OSHA publishes the revised Hazard Communication Standard.
  • May 19, 2026: chemical makers update labels and SDSs for substances.
  • Nov 20, 2026: employers update labels, the written program and training for substances, as necessary.
  • Nov 19, 2027: chemical makers update mixtures.
  • May 19, 2028: employers update for mixtures, as necessary.

Dates per the Federal Register rule of January 15, 2026 and OSHA's 1910.1200 text. States with their own OSHA plan may use different dates.
