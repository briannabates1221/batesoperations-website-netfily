About Bates Operations

Practical operations work starts with seeing the leak clearly.

Operational work has a way of looking fine from a distance. Up close, the weak points show up in handoffs, follow-up, vendor exceptions, and the small decisions that keep landing on the same person.

Brianna Bates is the founder of Bates Operations, where she built the Operational Leak Diagnosis framework to make operational pressure points easier to see before the answer becomes another tool, another hire, or more late-night coordination.

Her background combines retail banking, pharmacy operations, customer service, and household operations management. At Capital One, she processed 150+ daily transactions, balanced her cash drawer with zero discrepancies, and helped customers navigate online banking and service options. At Frisco Pharmacy, she verified coverage with 15+ insurers, resolved documentation discrepancies, processed copays, and maintained confidential records under regulated procedures.

She also manages schedules, budgets, records, appointments, vendor communications, and 20+ recurring monthly financial and administrative obligations. While completing a B.B.A. in Business Management at Western Governors University, she is developing practical process-improvement skills through SOPs and AI-assisted workflows. That mix of accuracy, follow-through, issue resolution, and deadline control is the practical lens behind the diagnosis framework.

Who Bates Operations is for

Boutique STR/MTR property managers and rental operators managing approximately 20-100 units.

The best fit is a property manager, rental operator, owner, office manager, or operations lead coordinating recurring turnovers, cleaners, maintenance vendors, and guest or owner follow-up with a small team. Work may still live across texts, inboxes, spreadsheets, and disconnected tools—and growth is starting to create staffing pressure.

Where revenue gets exposed

Small leaks become operating rules when nobody names them.

The work may still get done, but at a higher cost: more follow-up, slower resolution, more owner or guest escalations, and less room to grow without adding staff.

Follow-up loops

Teams lose time confirming status, completion, payment, access, and next steps across disconnected systems.

Unclear ownership

Tasks stall because the responsible person, current state, or escalation path is not obvious.

Vendor exceptions

Cleaning, maintenance, and turnover issues are often found after the guest, owner, or team already feels the impact.

Growth drag

Adding properties forces more coordination instead of more leverage when the operating system is not ready.
